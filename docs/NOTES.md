# Portfolio notes

Branch: `redesign/blue-hour`. The previous design (Stark / K.A.R.E.N. hologram desk, layout editor, GLB models) is still on `main`; read it with `git show main:<path>`.

## Concept
"Blue Hour": Huy's room at night, a glass corner studio on the top floor of a tower on Nob Hill, San Francisco, drawn live in WebGL behind the résumé. Visiting it opens like a game lobby: a terminal on black types a command, a split-flap board flips to SAN FRANCISCO, a queue finds a match and the board welcomes you to the arena, then you wake up in the street (eyelids blink open, blurred, then sharp), ride a lift up the face of the tower with a split-flap floor counter, and the room scans itself in around you at the top, with the name over it. After that it is a first-person walk through the room, with only light interface chrome (header clock, crosshair, callouts on the holograms). The earlier game heads-up display (round counter, minimap, time gauge) was removed on 2026-11-13 because it made the page read less like a portfolio. The desk carries the Stark idea from the old design: a projector on it throws a service graph and a route found across a city map, the left monitor is a scoreboard of the roles, and a projector on the lounge table throws the skills as an auto-battler board, one piece per skill. Scrolling walks the camera through seven chapters and moves the sky from dusk (19:42) through a moonlit night to sunrise (06:24) at Contact. Outside the glass is the bay as you would see it from there: the Golden Gate under the sunset behind the monitors, the Marin Headlands and Alcatraz to the north, Coit Tower and the Bay Bridge to the east, and the Transamerica Pyramid and Salesforce Tower against the sunrise at Contact. All content is server-rendered HTML over a fixed canvas, so the page reads with the scene switched off.

The game references are original artwork in the style of the genres. No game names, logos, maps, characters or interface copies, and nothing on the page claims a rank or a statistic that is not in `src/data/portfolio.ts`.

## Stack
Next.js 16.4 (Turbopack, cacheComponents), React 19.3, Tailwind v4 (preflight and `sr-only` only; the styling is plain CSS), three 0.186, @react-three/fiber 9, @react-three/postprocessing (Bloom, N8AO, custom grade).
`AGENTS.md` warns Next 16 has breaking changes. Read `node_modules/next/dist/docs/` before touching framework APIs.

## Layout
- `src/data/portfolio.ts`: all content, typed (`portfolio`, `stats`, `resumeUrl`, `portraitUrl`). Edit here, not in components.
- `src/lib/journey.ts`: the room plan (`ROOM`, `DESK`, `TABLE`, the projector `SITES`), the seven chapters (camera position, look target, field of view, hour), the intro (`LIFT` and `INTRO`, `introAt` for the camera, `cabinAt` for the lift's floor and doors, `revealAt` for how much of the room has been built) and the pure scroll-to-journey maths. `src/lib/live.ts`: the mutable store the page writes and the scene reads. `src/lib/emphasize.ts`: bolds the numbers in bullets.
- `src/components/Site.tsx` (client): header, clock, scroll and pointer wiring, the intro's flight, free look, error boundary around the scene. `src/components/Intro.tsx` (client): the intro's overlay and its matchmaking script (`SCRIPT`: edit the copy and timings there); `src/lib/flip.ts` is its split-flap maths. `src/components/Sections.tsx` (server): every section of the résumé. `src/app/layout.tsx` holds the one-line script that decides whether the intro plays.
- `src/scene/`: `sky-model.ts` (pure tables: sun, moon, sky colours, light levels per hour), `shared.ts` (shared uniforms and GLSL noise), `sky.ts` (sky dome and environment capture), `terrain.ts` (pure: where the landmarks, shoreline and hills are), `bay.ts` (water, land, both bridges, Alcatraz, Coit Tower, ridges, fog, and every far light with its reflection), `city.ts` (instanced houses on the street grid, our own tower under the room, downtown, the Pyramid and Salesforce Tower; one facade shader), `room.ts` (the room: oak floor with a planar mirror, glazing, sill, desk and its rig, lounge, lights, and the canvas textures for the monitors, rug and prints), `holograms.ts` (projectors and holograms, including the hex board: additive lines, points and plates sharing one shader), `grade.ts` (film response, vignette, light grain), `Scene.tsx` (canvas, frame loop, quality tiers).
- `src/app/globals.css`: design tokens and all page styles. `src/app/opengraph-image.tsx`: share image.
- `tests/journey.test.mjs`: assertions for the journey, the room (every camera stands inside it and clear of the furniture, projectors sit on furniture), the intro (the camera starts in the cabin, leaves it only through the open doorway, never walks and turns at once before the last move, stays at a walking pace, holds still while the room builds, lands exactly on the opening shot, the floors count up one at a time, the doors open only at the top floor, the room is built only once the camera is inside it) and the flip board (`flipPath`), the sky model, the panorama (landmarks on land, bridges over water, sun sets by the Gate) and the emphasis logic. Run with `npm test`.

## Decisions
- Scene objects are built imperatively at module scope and mounted with `<primitive>`. This keeps the React Compiler lint rules about mutating memoized values in `useFrame` out of the way.
- Everything in the scene is procedural (geometry, canvas textures, GLSL). There are no models or image textures to license or load.
- `frameloop="demand"` with a pacer: full rate while the visitor scrolls or moves, half rate when idle, no frames under reduced motion.
- Four quality tiers (`DPR`, `SAMPLES`, `MIRROR` in `Scene.tsx`). Every tier keeps antialiasing; only the best one has ambient occlusion. Quality steps down one way, and only after two consecutive 45-frame windows slower than 50 fps. Phones start one below the best.
- The panorama keeps the real order and rough compass bearings from Nob Hill and pulls the distances in, like a long lens (`PLACES` in `terrain.ts`, as bearing and distance). Downtown sits a little north of where it really is so it lands in the Contact shot.
- Far lights are points with a floor on their pixel size (`uDot`), so a lamp three kilometres away stays a clean dot instead of flickering. Each lamp over water gets a reflection streak drawn at its mirror point.
- Which hologram is up is a pure function of scroll position (`hologramsAt` in `journey.ts`); a projection builds upward from its emitter as its power goes 0 → 1. The hex board reads its pieces, colours and trait list from `portfolio.skills`; the page shows the same data as a trait list (`.traits` in `Sections.tsx`).
- The room is 8 m by 6.6 m with glass to the west, north and the north half of the east wall (`ROOM.split`), so the sunset, the bay and the sunrise each have a window. Sky light indoors falls off with distance from the glass (`indoor()` and `skylight()` in `room.ts`); the lamps, the cove strip and the screens carry the rest, and the lamps dim as the sun comes up.
- The intro is three beats on `<html data-intro>`: `open` (the terminal script, about 6.5 s, held until the scene has drawn its first frame so shader compiles happen out of sight) → `fly` (`INTRO.seconds` = 15.5 s, all driven by `live.intro` 0..1 through the table `BEATS` in `journey.ts`, in seconds: eyelids blink open inside a 3D lift while a blur sharpens; the floor indicator over its doors counts 1 to 11 while the camera hums; the doors part on the skeleton of the room; the camera walks straight out a step or two into the room, stops, and turns on the spot to take in all of it; it then holds still while the room is built, and when the build is nine tenths done it eases out of the hold and moves to the opening shot in one motion, walking and turning together, the name card over the build). Until that last move the camera never walks and turns at once (`introAt` is yaw/pitch keyframes, asserted in the test) and never exceeds a brisk walking pace → `land` (bars and page come back). The script in `layout.tsx` sets it before first paint, only on the first visit of a browser session (or any visit with `?intro` in the URL) with no hash and no reduced-motion preference, so it never shows without JavaScript or on a deep link. Any key, click, wheel or touch ends it at once; it also ends if the scene fails or has not come up in ten seconds. Quality tiers never step down during the flight, because a tier change rebuilds the composer and stalls a frame. For the same reason the first frames draw the holograms for real, behind the black card, so their shaders, textures and buffers are ready before the intro reveals them (revealed cold, they cost an 80 to 130 ms stall as the last move began; measured with a frame-time log in headless Chrome at 1x and 2x), walks ease in, cruise and ease out (`pace`) so none starts or stops with a jolt (asserted), and the pointer's look-around eases in after the intro instead of switching on in one frame. The blur layer's animation uses `backwards` fill so no backdrop filter stays live once it has finished.
- The lift (`scene/lift.ts`): a steel cabin built from primitives behind the room's south door (`LIFT` in `journey.ts` holds its place and size; it is the black door at x = 2.85). It is in the scene only until the camera has left it. It has no lights of its own, because adding or removing a light recompiles every material and stalls a frame: `lend` in `room.ts` moves the cove light nearest the door inside while the cabin is on screen, and the metals glow a little by themselves to fake bounce. The floor indicator is a canvas texture redrawn when the floor changes; the cabin's floor is a plane so the room floor's mirror pass can see through it.
- Materialize (`room.ts`, `materialize`): at load every object in the room gets a twin made of its edges (instanced meshes baked into one geometry), the skeleton, shown as glowing blue lines while the build runs. Every room material, and one shared shadow-depth material, is patched (`growing`) to drop the fragments of world-space cells (5 cm) that are not built yet: a cell's moment is half random and half its height, so the room grows from the floor up, a cell glows blue for a short band before it settles, and the lines fade as the solids take over. Because the discard is in the depth pass too, shadows and ambient occlusion stay right. The floor, the slab under it, the glass and the shader materials are left out. `uBuild` is shared by all of them; at rest it is 1 and nothing is discarded.
- The split-flap board (`Intro.tsx`, `lib/flip.ts`): a tile reaches a character only through the ones before it on the drum (`flipPath`), 3 to 7 flaps, varying by tile so a row settles raggedly. Each flap is two Web Animations (the old top half falls, the new bottom half lands) over `STEP` = 120 ms. The board has a fixed 20 tiles; the floor counter reuses the same tiles.
- Callouts (`TAGS` in `Scene.tsx`) are DOM links the scene pins to its holograms each frame; clicking one jumps to its section. One shows only while its hologram is up, in frame with room for its label, right of the text column, which the camera chapters are aimed to allow at the opening, Projects, Skills and Contact. They live in the backdrop, so `main` and its sections ignore the pointer and only the text columns (`.hero-card`, `.col`) take it. Desktop only.
- Two accents with separate jobs: ember for content that matters (numbers, awards, times), hologram blue (`--holo`) for interface chrome (brackets, callouts).
- Portrait screens get re-framed shots (`tall` on a chapter: the opening and Skills), because the page text covers the lower half of the screen there.
- Reduced motion cuts between chapters instead of travelling, and stops ambient animation.
- If WebGL or the scene module fails, the error boundary removes the canvas and the CSS gradient on `.backdrop` stands in.
- Deploy target: Vercel, already live at `huyhg.dev`.

## Gotchas
- With `frameloop="demand"`, calling `invalidate()` once per animation frame draws only every other frame (30 fps on a 60 Hz screen). The pacer asks for two frames (`invalidate(2)`) while busy. The old single call made the slow-frame monitor think the GPU was weak and drop to 1× resolution with no antialiasing within seconds, which is why the backdrop used to look soft.
- The camera far plane is 40 km and near is 0.25 m. Do not pull near in further or distant geometry starts to z-fight.
- Glow, reflection streaks and fog use negative `renderOrder` so the holograms always draw in front of them.
- R3F 9.8 builds its own `THREE.Clock`, so the "THREE.Clock deprecated" console warning is expected.
- Shaders that include `AIR_GLSL` must not redeclare `uSunDir`, `uZenith`, `uHorizon` or `uGlow`.
- An additive pass must never output NaN (for example `pow` of a varying that interpolates below zero): one bad pixel goes through the bloom blur and turns the whole frame black.
- The dev server can serve stale CSS for a moment after an edit; reload before judging a screenshot. Running `npm run build` while `next dev` is up can leave the dev server on an old stylesheet until it is restarted; `npx next start -p 3100` previews the build instead. The production build has also been seen to keep an old stylesheet through several rebuilds after a CSS edit; if a style change does not show, check the served `.css` for it before debugging the style.
- The mirror pass for the floor hides the floor and the slab under it; anything new that sits below y = 0 has to be hidden there too or it shows up in the reflection.
- `filter` on an element disables its own `backdrop-filter`. The header backing (`.bar::before`) relies on `backdrop-filter`.

## Verified (2026-11-13)
`npm test`, `npx eslint src tests`, `npx tsc --noEmit` and `npm run build` pass (fully static). Headless Chrome with GPU: all seven chapters at 1440×900, the opening and Skills at 1920×1080, phone layout at 390×800 and 375×740 and a tall 820×1180 window with no horizontal scroll, reduced motion (no intro, cuts between chapters), free look (toggle, time slider, Esc), a callout click, and the fallback with WebGL contexts disabled (the intro ends at once and the page shows). Intro (reworked 2026-11-13 into the matchmaking terminal, wake-up, a 3D lift, the room's skeleton and a voxel build): checked only as still frames from real-time headless Chrome at 1440×900, every beat from the typed command to the landed hero, with no console errors; the full run is about 22 s. Not yet measured for frame rate, not seen on a phone or in Safari and Firefox, and the flap and blink motion has been judged from stills only. Frame rate over seven seconds of continuous scrolling on an Apple-silicon laptop: 59 fps at 1440×900, 58 fps at 3024 px wide, 52 fps at 3840 px wide, with resolution held at 2× throughout. The only console output is the expected `THREE.Clock` warning. Not tested on a real phone, a low-end GPU, or in Safari and Firefox.
